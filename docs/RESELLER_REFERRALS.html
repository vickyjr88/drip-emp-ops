Product & technical proposal

A referral program for resellers

Shareable affiliate links, transparent dual pricing, and a commission paid on the margin between retail and reseller price — built on the pricing tier the shop already has, not a parallel system.

For Drip Emporium Status Proposal — Phase 1 in progress Touches Storefront, checkout, customer portal, ledger

01How it works, end to end

A reseller gets one link. Sharing it wins them either a commission on a stranger's purchase, or a lower price on a purchase they place themselves — the same mechanic serving two of the flows asked for.

1 Reseller Generates a link from their account. One reseller can hold several — one per campaign, if they want to see which one actually converts.
2 Shopper Opens the link, browses and adds to cart. A cookie remembers who referred them for a set attribution window.
3 System At checkout, the order is stamped with the referring reseller. Shopper still pays retail — nothing changes for them.
4 System On payment, a commission line is posted: retail minus reseller price, per unit sold.
5 Reseller Sees the commission accrue on their dashboard, and gets paid out on the shop's normal schedule.

The second flow — a reseller buying as themselves, on behalf of a client standing next to them or messaging on WhatsApp — skips the link and the commission entirely. They just check out logged in, and pay reseller price directly. That's a more direct margin than a commission would give them, which is exactly why it should stay simple rather than routed through the referral machinery.

02What's already there to build on

This is not a system starting from nothing. Customer.priceTier already distinguishes RETAIL / RESELLER / WHOLESALE, and every ProductVariant already carries a resellerPriceKes alongside retail. The margin this program pays out is a number the schema already knows how to compute.

One gap changes the shape of the build, though: the public storefront checkout never looks at price tier today. Tier pricing only applies when staff create an order by hand, or when stock goes out on consignment. A logged-in reseller shopping the website right now pays full retail regardless of their tier — so before dual pricing or referral commissions can exist online, checkout itself needs to learn what a logged-in shopper's tier is and price accordingly. That's not extra scope this program invented; it's a prerequisite the storefront was always going to need.

03Dual pricing, done transparently

Once checkout is tier-aware, showing both prices to a logged-in reseller is a display decision, not a new pricing engine — the numbers already exist per variant.

  • Product cards and the product page show retail with reseller struck through or shown alongside, visible only when the viewer is logged in as a reseller.
  • The margin itself — "you keep KES 800 on this pair" — is worth stating outright rather than making the reseller do the subtraction. It's the entire pitch of the program, restated at the moment it matters most.
  • A retail shopper, or anyone not logged in, sees exactly what they see today. Nothing changes for them.

04The two real decisions

Everything else in this proposal follows a default path. These two choices don't have a single obviously-correct answer — they trade real convenience against real risk, and are worth deciding deliberately rather than defaulting into.

Attribution: how long does a link's credit last?

Recommended

30-day cookie, last click wins

Industry-standard window. If two resellers' links are both clicked before a purchase, the most recent one gets the credit — simplest rule to explain and to dispute.

Trade-off: a reseller who does the early convincing can lose the sale to whoever sent the last link, even a cheeky reseller re-sharing someone else's find.

First click wins

Whoever introduced the shopper first keeps the credit even if a second link is clicked later.

Trade-off: harder to explain, and rewards the first link even if it did nothing to actually close the sale.

Payout: settled per sale, or batched?

Recommended

Accrue continuously, pay out monthly

Commission posts to a running balance the moment an order is paid and past any return window. The shop pays out on one predictable date, the same way payroll already runs.

Trade-off: a reseller waits up to a month to actually receive money they can already see accruing.

Pay per order, on settlement

Each commission pays out the moment its order clears returns — money moves faster and more often.

Trade-off: many small payments instead of one clean batch — more transaction fees, more reconciliation work for staff, for the same total paid out.

05A worked example

Air Max 90, EUR 42. Retail KES 8,999, reseller price KES 7,500. A referred shopper buys two pairs.

Commission on this order

Retail price × 2 pairs KES 17,998
Reseller price × 2 pairs KES 15,000
Shopper actually pays KES 17,998 (retail — unchanged)
Commission owed to the reseller + KES 2,998

Compare the second flow: the same reseller, buying those two pairs themselves to hand to a client, checks out logged in and simply pays KES 15,000 — the margin is theirs immediately, no commission step, no waiting for payout.

Ordered so each phase is useful on its own and unlocks the next — not an arbitrary split of one big feature.

Resolve the logged-in customer's priceTier at checkout, the same way staff-created orders already do. Show dual pricing to resellers on the storefront. No links, no commissions yet — this alone lets a reseller log in and buy on behalf of a client at reseller price, one of the flows asked for.

07Guardrails worth building in from day one

Self-referral

A reseller must not be able to earn a commission by buying through their own link — that's a discount wearing a commission's clothes, and it's the first thing anyone testing the system will try. Block it at the point an order is stamped with a referrer, not after the fact.

Returns and refunds

A commission should not be finalized the instant an order is paid — it should wait out whatever return window the shop already honors. A refunded order needs to claw the commission back before it's ever paid out, not after.

Reseller pricing on offer stock

Offer markdowns are explicitly retail-only today, by design — a promotional cut is never meant to erode reseller margin further. Keep that rule: a referred sale on offer stock still commissions off the standing reseller price, not the discounted retail price.

08Worth adding that wasn't asked for

Per-link stats, not just a per-reseller totallow effort

If a reseller can create more than one link, showing clicks and sales per link — not just a lump sum — is what turns this from "I get paid sometimes" into something they can actually optimize their posting around.

A minimum payout thresholdprevents fee waste

Without one, a KES 200 commission triggers the same payout transaction cost as a KES 20,000 one. Carry small balances forward to the next cycle instead.

Tiered commission rates, laterdefer

Nothing here stops a top-performing reseller eventually earning a better rate than a new one. Worth naming now so the schema doesn't have to be reshaped to add it — but not worth building before the flat-rate version has actually run for a while.

Fraud-shaped monitoringcheap insurance

A handful of orders from the same device or address, all through one reseller's link, all just under a return-window deadline, is a pattern worth a staff alert — not blocking anything automatically, just surfacing it.
