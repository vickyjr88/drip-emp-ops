Smart-casual sits in the gap between a full official shoe and a sneaker, and it's the category most people get wrong — either too dressy for the setting or not put-together enough. Here's how to actually use it.

What "smart-casual" really means for shoes

A smart-casual shoe has more structure and a cleaner finish than a sneaker, but without the full formality of a laced official — think a refined leather-look upper on a slightly more relaxed shape. It's built for settings that aren't strictly formal but still expect more than sportswear.

When it's the right call

A dinner that isn't black-tie, a casual Friday at an office with a smart-casual dress code, a date, or a social event where jeans feel too relaxed but a suit feels like overkill — this is exactly where a smart-casual shoe earns its place. It reads as making an effort without looking like you're dressed for the office.

Pairing it with the rest of the outfit

Smart-casual shoes pair naturally with chinos or tailored denim rather than joggers or cargos — the shoe sets the tone, and the rest of the outfit should match that slightly elevated register. A plain shirt or a simple knit finishes the look without overdressing it.

Smart-casual vs a laceless official

The two categories overlap, but a laceless official still reads more formal and works better with a full suit, while a genuine smart-casual shoe is built to be worn without a jacket at all. If you're only ever dressing for the office, officials are the safer buy; if you're dressing for everything in between, smart-casuals fill that gap.

One pair covers most situations

A single black or dark-toned smart-casual pair covers the vast majority of situations this category is built for — you don't need multiple colours the way you might with sneakers. Get the fit and finish right on one pair before considering a second.
