Ask a Nairobian where to buy shoes in town and Ronald Ngala Street comes up fast. It's one of the CBD streets known for footwear, with shops, malls and traders selling everything from school shoes to the latest sneakers. That choice is a good thing for a buyer, but it also means it pays to know how to shop it.

This guide is for anyone planning a trip to Ronald Ngala Street for sneakers, whichever shop you end up buying from. Full disclosure: Drip Emporium is on this street, in Dubai Merchants Mall, so we see every day what makes a good buying trip and what doesn't.

Before you leave home

  • Know your size. Many sneakers in Kenya are sold in EUR sizes (we stock EUR 36 to 46). If you're unsure, measure your foot against a ruler in the evening, when feet are largest, and check our sneaker size guide.
  • Decide what you want. A rough idea of the model, colour and budget saves time and keeps you from being talked into something else.
  • Go in daylight, and travel light. The CBD is busy. Keep your phone and wallet secure, and carry only what you need.
  • Charge your phone. You'll want it for M-Pesa, for photos of what you're comparing, and for looking up a model.

Compare before you commit

Walk the street and look at the same model in two or three places before buying. Ask for the price on the pair itself, then compare like with like: same model, same colourway, same size. A pair that is much cheaper than everywhere else deserves a question, not a purchase. Our guide to spotting fake sneakers explains what to look at.

Always try both shoes on

One shoe on the shelf tells you very little. Ask to try on both, lace them up properly, and walk a few steps. Check that:

  • the size label inside matches what you asked for;
  • both shoes are the same size, shape and colour;
  • there's room for your toes, and nothing pinches across the widest part of your foot;
  • the sole is clean and the stitching is even.

At Drip Emporium you can try pairs on and pay only if they fit. Whichever shop you choose, make that your rule: don't pay for shoes you haven't worn.

Prices and bargaining

Many traders in Nairobi expect some discussion on price. A fair approach is to ask the marked price first, say what you've seen elsewhere, and see whether there's room. Be wary of a price that collapses the moment you hesitate: it makes it hard to know what a fair price actually is, so go back to comparing the same pair elsewhere.

Paying safely

  • Confirm the total before you pay, and confirm it again on your M-Pesa prompt.
  • Check the business or till name shown before you enter your PIN.
  • Never share your M-Pesa PIN or a one-time code with anyone, including someone who says they are helping you pay.
  • Keep the M-Pesa confirmation message and, where you can, a receipt.

Ask about exchanges before you pay

Wrong size? Ask what the shop does about it before you hand over money. At Drip Emporium, unworn shoes in their original box are the easiest to exchange, and we'd rather swap a size than leave you with shoes you won't wear. Whatever the shop's policy is, get it clearly, keep the box, and keep the receipt.
