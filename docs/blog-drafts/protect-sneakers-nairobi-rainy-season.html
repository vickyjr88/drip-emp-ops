Nairobi has two rainy seasons: the long rains, roughly March to May, and the short rains, roughly October to December. If you're wearing sneakers around the CBD right now, you've probably already met a puddle or two. Water doesn't have to ruin a good pair, but a few habits make a big difference.

Wear the right pair for the weather

Different materials cope with wet very differently:

  • Smooth leather handles splashes well and wipes clean, and is a good rainy-day choice.
  • Suede and nubuck mark easily and can stain and stiffen when soaked. Keep them for dry days.
  • White and light mesh soak up dirty water and show every mark. They're better saved for sunny days.
  • Waterproof membrane pairs, such as some Gore-Tex models, are made for wet weather. Ask us if you're looking for one.

Chunkier soles with deep tread also grip wet pavements better than flat, smooth ones. That's worth thinking about on the busy pavements of town.

Protect them before you go out

A waterproofing or protector spray gives the surface a head start against water and stains. Test it on a small hidden area first, spray in a ventilated place, let it dry fully before wearing, and reapply every few weeks in rainy season. Suede needs a spray made for suede.

Dry them properly (and don't rush it)

How you dry a wet pair matters more than how you wash it. Do this:

  1. Take out the laces and insoles so they dry separately.
  2. Stuff the shoes loosely with plain paper or paper towels to draw out the water. Avoid newspaper on light-coloured pairs, because the ink can transfer.
  3. Change the paper after a few hours when it's soaked, until the inside is dry.
  4. Leave them somewhere ventilated, out of direct sun.

Avoid heaters, hairdryers and a hot car. Strong heat can warp the shape, crack leather and weaken the glue that holds soles on. Never put sneakers in a tumble dryer.

Clean the mud once it's dry

Let mud dry first, then brush it off with a soft brush. Wipe what's left with a damp cloth and a little mild soap. For more, see our guides to cleaning white sneakers and caring for leather and suede.

Condition leather afterwards

Leather that's been wet and dried can feel stiff. Once the pair is fully dry, a little leather conditioner keeps it supple and helps prevent cracking.

Rotate your pairs

Wearing the same pair every day in wet weather never gives it time to dry out, which wears it faster and can leave it smelling. Having two or three pairs to alternate lets each one dry for a full day or two. We explain how to build a small collection without overspending in our rotation guide.

Don't store them damp

A damp pair shut in a box or a bag can grow mould and smell. Make sure shoes are completely dry before you put them away.
